# Campaign Case Candidates

Candidates for the opening case and early campaign, grounded in published, real treatments. Criteria for an opening case:


1. A treatment chain where each step depends on the one before
2. A published treatment record, ideally with before/after images
3. Unambiguous conditions (save ambiguity for later cases)
4. Examinable with rank 1 tools, with at most one unlock
5. Lower stakes (legitimate peripheral participation), but a good story

All four candidates below are from the Smithsonian American Art Museum (SAAM), treated at its Lunder Conservation Center, and have CC0 main images in Smithsonian Open Access (checked via the API). The game ships only the post-treatment image; before-treatment images on SAAM's pages are used as design reference only.


## Candidates

### 1. Hubert Vos, *Alice Barney in White Satin*, 1894 (opener, confirmed)

* Record: https://americanart.si.edu/collections/search/artwork/?id=25783
* Source: SAAM blog, "Spring Cleaning for Alice Pike Barney" (2011): https://americanart.si.edu/blog/eye-level/2011/12/835/spring-cleaning-alice-pike-barney
* Conditions: surface dirt; yellowed natural resin varnish masking the dress's warm and cool pastel tones; small losses along the edge.
* Treatment (in order): testing; surface dirt removed with a pH-balanced water-based solution; discolored varnish removed with a solvent gel; new non-yellowing synthetic varnish; small edge losses inpainted with a removable varnish-based conservation paint.
* Why it fits: a clean, sequential chain. Reversibility is explicit in the source (the inpainting can be removed by the next conservator). UV has a real role (natural resin varnish). Treated by a Kress Fellow, an early-career conservator, which mirrors the apprentice. The sitter, Alice Pike Barney, was an artist and patron with a story of her own.

### 2. John Ritto Penniman, *Christ Tempted by the Devil*, 1818

* Record: https://americanart.si.edu/collections/search/artwork/?id=19532
* Source: SAAM "Before and After Conservation Treatment": https://americanart.si.edu/art/conservation/before-after
* Conditions: active flaking and paint loss down to the bare wood panel; discolored varnish; grime.
* Treatment (in order): loose and flaking paint consolidated with adhesive; varnish and grime removed with solvents; losses filled and inpainted.
* Why it fits: the fullest chain, and the strongest "order matters" lesson. Cleaning before consolidating would lose original paint, an irreversible consequence. Good second case.

### 3. Alfred Thompson Bricher, *Castle Rock, Marblehead*, 1878

* Record: https://americanart.si.edu/collections/search/artwork/?id=2777
* Source: SAAM before/after page (above)
* Conditions (seen in raking light): severely curled cracking; paint detaching from the canvas; discolored varnish; embedded grime.
* Treatment: cracking relaxed with moisture and weights, then stabilized; varnish and grime removed.
* Why it fits: introduces structural work. Raking light is central. A candidate for the mentor modeling an above-rank treatment.

### 4. William Frederick De Haas, *Afternoon on Saco Bay, Coast of Maine*, 1874

* Record: https://americanart.si.edu/collections/search/artwork/?id=35353
* Source: SAAM before/after page (above)
* Conditions (seen in raking light): canvas buckled and out of plane; overall cracking; discolored varnish with embedded grime.
* Treatment: fabric relaxed with moisture and weights, then stabilized; varnish and grime removed.
* Why it fits: similar to Bricher; an alternative or a later infinite-mode reference.


## Possible early campaign arc


1. **Vos:** a clean, reversible chain; learn examination, testing, cleaning, and varnish.
2. **Penniman:** order matters; consequences of sequencing.
3. **Bricher:** structural damage; mentor models the above-rank treatment.
4. **Van Gogh, *Wheat Field with Cypresses*:** a famous work in excellent condition; restraint and ethical judgment (see `pilot_case_wheat_field.md`).
